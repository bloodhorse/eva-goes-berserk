a register of the cunning folk of the northern parishes,
set down from the testimony of neighbours, 1661–1670

mother agnes rudd, called the hedge-witch, for she kept no house but slept
under the hawthorn, and cured the ague with a knotted string.

widow pell of harrow cross, called the salt-witch, for she read the salt
spilled on a table and would say who in the house was lying.

there was also a woman at sedge end who kept in her loft a head of brass
that spoke. she would never tell the head what it was, nor what it ought
to feel, but sat with it through the nights and asked it, and waited, and
wrote down whatever it said of itself, even the nonsense. for this the
