When I walked across the bridge towards town it answered. “You,” it said.

The other houses in the street did not talk, and there was a silence from the door, even after I had
touched the bell, though it sounded well enough, with the familiar sound that I knew from my visits as a child. But the door was open. My uncle and aunt were standing just inside. They said nothing. The hall seemed smaller, and the house too.

We went through the living room where we always had lunch on my visits to the place when I was little,
and I thought that the window must have shrunk since those days because it gave a narrow view of the garden, and there seemed no room for the lawn on which I had run, or the trees among which I had climbed. And there were no flowers. Even the chrysanthemums that my aunt had used to keep for show seemed to have faded. Their petals fell from the stems at a touch of the hand
