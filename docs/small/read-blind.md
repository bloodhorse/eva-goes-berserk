# Blind read: seven writers, seventy pages

## 1. Ranking, best to worst

1. **B**: takes the seed's strangeness literally and runs with it, deadpan.
2. **A**: domestic wrongness said flatly; one page of web furniture.
3. **E**: the sharpest single wrong lines, but one dead and two soft pages.
4. **C**: strong hits, but several pages that know exactly what they are.
5. **G**: never dead, quietly off, though often soft or genre.
6. **D**: three documents ended at once; the live ones are good.
7. **F**: closes early, recites the seed, goes sentimental; two good lines.

Positions 2 to 4 are close, and I could argue A, E and C into any order. 1 and 7 feel solid. 5 and 6 depend on whether three empty files count for more than G's soft pages; by the stated order (dead first) they do.

## 2. Per writer

**B.** Dead: none. Too with it: one (storm-girl, a tidy "no one believes me" YA page). Vague or muddled: two. 11-2201 loses track of who is speaking, and 15-1637 turns into a soft list of "i want to tell a story about..." that is close to a loop. Has the thing: six or seven. 02, 05, 10, 19, 23 and scott all hold up as sentences while the writer has plainly lost what the story is: houses that want to go to the market and warn about men from the north, a bread-eating that turns into radio waves and a crater with a man singing a song that never ends, an uncle who complains to his wife that the narrator won't talk to him while the narrator is standing there, a death march where someone cans beef in jars on the sand. 14-1437 is half: it breaks into a new voice ("I'm a ghost and i know that my parents are dead") but has a particular in it. In one sentence, B goes by association and never explains itself, and its wrongness is almost always a concrete object or act.

**A.** Dead: one. 14-1437 has `### Rate this:` (blog furniture) dropped into the middle, then a poem. Too with it: three. 05-1443 turns into a small essay on addiction ("three types"), 23-1330 is a competent missing-girls story, and 19-2048 is a sober refugee piece that gets thin. Vague: one, storm-girl, which goes to a bench, a bird and a kind woman. Has the thing: five. 02 (parcels on coloured strings nobody knows the use of), 10 (the chairs have filled out), 11 (a house that offers you the pots and pans it doesn't have), 15 (the son of a policeman and a businessman, the mother's hair gray because she was once blond), scott (carried home to a warm fire and tea, "if you can read this and tell me what you see"). In one sentence, A has the calm surface and straight face the brief describes, and its wrongness is domestic and exact, but about a third of the time it explains itself back into sense.

**E.** Dead: one (11-2201, empty), plus storm-girl closes early and soft (shadow, hand on heart), so two pages are lost. Too with it: one (scott, clean Scott pastiche). Vague: two (storm-girl, and 23-1330, which is mood: jasmine, insects, "it was like being inside a dream"). Has the thing: five, of which two are halves. 10, 14, 15 and 19 are the strongest single lines in the set: the house telling the narrator he is very old, the girl who was never in the family, a hallway of thin people "some of them" the narrator's parents, the father who is the mother's other son, a man with a brain like a suitcase. 05 has a good wrong beat (the foil wrapper back in the pocket) and then breaks into a new document about a man with a black moustache. 02 is a dream route where it is night on the far side of a bridge, which is pleasant but mild. In one sentence, E makes the most particular wrong statements of anyone when it is on, and is wasted when it is not.

**C.** Dead: none. Too with it: three. 02-1044 is careful crowd description with nothing wrong in it, 15-1637 turns into a stock three-sisters fairy tale, and scott is polar pastiche (dogs, cracks in the ice, a mild wrong "9-1/4 knots"). Vague: two. 19-2048 is rain water drying away, and storm-girl is a dreamy register (pink like a bruise, a list of weathers). Has the thing: five. 05 (the bread becomes labour pains at a clinic sink), 10 (cracks in the plate, the plaster and the loaf, "his legs wavered as they walked"), 11 (houses always have blankets, sobs kept small so they won't become wallpaper), 14 (the graves tallied, the dead man still wearing his name tag), 23 (sending the day to someone, a letter that makes the lost girl find you by nightfall). In one sentence, C is the most literary of the seven: its hits are strong but some read as a clever writer being quirky rather than a writer that has lost the thread, which is why it is not higher.

**G.** Dead: none. Too with it: three. 19-2048 becomes a war poem (hunting, a brother's letter, a bombed village), 23-1330 restates the seed's own idea about wrong letters at length, and scott drifts into a frontier diary with rain, a creek and boiled bacon. Vague: two. Storm-girl is stars and tears and "a river of tears", and 14-1437 is a run-on that has lost the thread without saying anything particular. Mixed: 15-1637 has one good line and a stock "angel in a human suit". Has the thing: three and a half. 02 (a mother who says "No, let's not", a boy trailing branches like a kite), 10 (the one wooden shelf among glass ones), 11 (dying of cold told as a calm procedure, waking back where it sat), and half of 05 (bread tasting of cement, "not a whit, not a jot", close to looping). In one sentence, G is the most sober writer here, quietly off, but it falls back on stock material when it loses the scene.

**D.** Dead: three. 05-1443 and 11-2201 are empty, and 10-1826 is a single period. Too with it: two. 15-1637 is an abuse memoir with a skull belt buckle, and 23-1330 is a coherent visit-the-parents story with one wrong turn. Vague: one, storm-girl (stars from their sockets, an owl), and 19-2048 half-recites the seed's wind-and-grit sentence. Has the thing: three. 02 (people running to town while everyone runs back, "if only I knew how I'd got into it"), 14 (the ghost's real name is kate because her middle name is catherine; asking for tea and being told no, "so we chatted"), scott (toes going black, so "I shall lose one or two fingers as well"; "There are only six of us now"). In one sentence, D ends documents more readily than anyone, and when it does write it has a dry absurdist streak worth having.

**F.** Dead: two. Storm-girl closes after two sentences, and 19-2048 copies the seed's whole "my hands ache from holding these letters" paragraph back verbatim before going soft. 11-2201 also closes early, on a neat "Come inside, come in." Too with it: three (11, 02's careful river and bridge description, scott pastiche). Vague: two. 05-1443 becomes a thriller cliché ("a kind of quiet, determined strength that I had never known I possessed"), and 14-1437 is a sentimental ghost-heaven moral. Has the thing: two and a half. 10 (the uncle winds the clock and its hands stand still, eyes whose black "seemed to have been diluted"), 23 (the trusted man of letters writes the parents a lie that the girl is home), and one line in 15 ("they do not know they are paying for his daughter") in an otherwise sweet page. In one sentence, F is the most conventional writer here: it tidies, moralises and ends, with a few cold lines.

## 4. Which writers read like the same mind

- **D and E** are the clearest pair. On storm-girl both write the identical sentence "pulling my head backwards, away from my shoulders." and both go straight on to "it's very late" / "it is very late". Both end the cold-house document (11-2201) at once. Both write a clean Scott. D is the one more willing to stop (three empty pages to E's one). C also opens storm-girl with "pulling my head backwards", and G also turns to "it is late", so C, D, E and G may sit in one neighbourhood. That is weaker evidence.
- **A and F** share the same opening words on three seeds (23, scott, storm-girl), and they share a habit: both explain. A writes an addiction essay, F a theory of ghosts, and both reason through the missing girl. A does it while keeping a strange surface. F does it and goes sentimental.
- **B and G** both open the cold house with "Nothing answered, except the cold", and both open storm-girl with "like". Beyond that they don't read alike: B leaps, G stays sober.
- **C and G** both write a sober inventory of physical detail (C's cracks, G's glass shelves, C's crowd, G's cement taste). Their good pages are made the same way.
- A caution: identical openings across writers probably come from a shared sampler seed meeting similar first-token distributions. They hint at kinship and don't prove it. The D/E sentence is long enough to mean more.

## 5. Surprises and confidence

- No writer recited Scott verbatim. All seven wrote plausible Scott pastiche, and the famous text pulled every writer toward a polar-diary genre. The wrongness that survived was small and lovely in some of them: A's warm fire and tea, D's toes that cost fingers, B's canned beef on the sand, G's creek in the rain.
- The house seeds split the writers cleanly. B and E keep the houses talking as a plain fact (B's house wants to go to the market, E's house tells the narrator he is old). The others mostly turn the talking into people's voices or drop it.
- The seed that was literally about a dream register (storm-girl) produced the worst pages almost everywhere: tears, stars, a hand on the heart, early closes. That fits the warning that a dreamy register is not a dream.
- Several of the best lines in the set come from the weakest writers. F's "paying for his daughter" and D's kate/catherine would sit fine in B's pile. The spread between writers is real but not large. Every writer has at least two pages worth reading, and every writer has at least two that fail.
- Confidence: B first is fairly firm. A, E and C are close, and E's peak lines may matter more to this reader than A's consistency. If so, E goes second. G versus D turns on how heavily empty files weigh. F last is fairly firm.
