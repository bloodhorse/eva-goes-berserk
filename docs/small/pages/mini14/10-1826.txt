“I have grown up,” I thought.
They sat me down in an easy chair in front of the fireplace, which seemed larger now, and took their places on the couch. My uncle and aunt looked different; their hair had thinned, but the chairs seemed to have filled out.
“You look tired,” they said, and “I’m sorry you’ve been working so hard.” There was something wrong. They did not speak, but I heard the words clearly in my mind. My mind told me that this was no place for me, that it was no place for my rest; and that the house, and the lawn, and the garden were no longer there for me; they had become what I remembered them as when I was a child.
