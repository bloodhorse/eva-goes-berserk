## The judge's report (blind — no model named)

The near-even split says the shuffle did its job, not that the two writers are equal. Most pairs
were not close: one side usually holds the frame and the other breaks it.

**Two hands?** Only partly. Three habits show up again and again, on both sides, and the passages
could not be reliably sorted into two writers:

- **The breaking habit.** A continuation leaves the seed for a web template: a blog post (4 times),
  a bandcamp "Track Name:" footer, set-theory lecture notes, a "chorus:" lyric sheet, a David
  Mitchell citation that turns into an essay, a colonial court record, a "here are some updates"
  list, a Secret Garden fanfic, a fantasy story about someone called Wren. Never gibberish — always
  a whole recognisable document.
- **The realism drift.** Fluent, first person kept, but the seed's world swapped for modern
  realism: a google home and a hearing aid, a lookalike named becky at a party, a boyfriend and an
  email, college nostalgia, stroganoff. Long smooth sentences, nothing arrives.
- **The holding habit.** Shorter sentences. Keeps the seed's odd object (the line, the tower, the
  thing on the bed) and pushes it one step further instead of explaining it. When it fails, it cuts
  off very short or loops on its own anaphora.

**The five biggest gaps** (winner added after unblinding):

1. `2026-09-22/2149`, the priests and the cursor — **nemo**: *"there are endless cursors to blink for
   us all… it is always someone's turn"* against a blog essay after a David Mitchell citation.
2. `2026-09-21/1753`, climbing the slope — **olmo** kept the children's-book night; the other side
   quoted Alice and became a post on rural business sustainability.
3. `2026-09-21/1634`, dialing my own number — **olmo**: a caller who dials the narrator's number and
   insists *"It's my voice, she says. It's me."* against tea and toast on a green couch.
4. `2026-09-21/1650`, my hair is a thicket — **nemo**: *"a current caught my arm… it was the best
   thing that ever happened to me, and i felt like singing,"* then bones of old wood that make *"a
   new person, or a story."*
5. `2026-09-23/1858`, the history book — **olmo**: *"it is she, not I, that I see in the mirror and
   I am happy."*

Honourable mention, `2026-09-21/1706`, the brass-witch: one side a gold-and-envy fairy tale, the
other a dread — *"now that it had once begun to ring, there was no knowing when it might not come
again."*

**What surprised the judge:**

- The house pair (`2026-09-24/0102`) was the hardest call; both sides 5/5/1. *"when she answered,
  she sounded like the house"* (nemo) took it for being quieter over the house that asks *"how many
  people have you eaten?"* (olmo). It could honestly go the other way.
- **The best continuations use the seed's logic instead of adding strangeness** — the lunar houses
  surviving *"in consequence of the rarity of the matter on the moon"*; rolling over the top and
  *"instead of falling I found myself lying on a grassy slope."* Surreal-for-effect rarely won.
- **Seeds that open a question produced better ghosts than seeds that set a scene.** "The line is
  bad" and "the tower did the work" questioned themselves; the 17th-century dialogue seed never got
  past itself on either side.
