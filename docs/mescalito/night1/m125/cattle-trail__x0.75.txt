The incident was too unexpected for us to take any concerted action; nevertheless we galloped in opposite directions around the stampede and brought them up in the best shape we could in so short a time, when, finding the danger to have passed, we returned to bed. The incident taught us to exercise a more vigilant supervision over the herd and to keep more alert while on guard.

But we had learned another important lesson, namely, that cattle do not necessarily run because of fear, but when stampeded by circumstances beyond their control.

In our home range, a portion of the Atascosa was our nearest approach to a river, and here the cattle found most abundant supplies of food, grass being exceedingly lush. This region was the haunt of many of our early friends who owned cattle herds on its banks
