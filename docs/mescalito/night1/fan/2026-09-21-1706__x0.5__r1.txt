 Once when
she was asked if she liked brass, she said "No," but her husband said he did
not mind either way. "What's your problem with brass?" people said, but they
were only being polite, since it was a polite society; their problems were not
with brass but with each other, and what the neighbours called "brass". They
did not like brass.

But what is brass?

"Brass," the woman said, "is an alloy of copper and zinc." But her husband said
"Brass, as I see it, is a thing of beauty, and a joy for all the ages." This,
of course, was true. For all the neighbours agreed that brass, while an
alloy, was not merely an alloy; it was something else entirely: a brass thing,
