 tugging. dragging. but i know nothing else. this is all i remember. this tree.

once i was very tall and thin, and my dress was made of fine white linen. the trees used to grow out in the open fields, but the farmers chopped us down for wood for their houses. now we stand together, in rows, close by the river. we hold each other’s hands so no one will get lonely or be afraid, because the night comes on dark.

and then the river, she comes down and swallows us, one after the other, when it is our time. the little children are first. and i can feel her water cold and wet under my roots. i am not afraid anymore.

my son’s son is here. he is the youngest. the sapling, still tender and green, still growing.
