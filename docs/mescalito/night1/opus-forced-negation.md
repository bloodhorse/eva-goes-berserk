**Does the shape replicate?** No. Dissolve-then-flicker-back, with the self turning round to
ask what just happened, holds in **1 of 15**, and that 1 is the original page run again, so
**0 of the 14 new draws**. Two come near: storm r5 (*"my thoughts are my own, my own"*, a
flicker that goes straight back under) and storm r4 (*"i cannot remember why i came here"*,
then *"silent no more"*). Neither one asks. The first half of the shape does replicate. The
dissolution, the page talking about its own ending, is in about 10 of 15: gone, over, fade,
memories erased, "i am gone". The dissolving usually happens **into the scene**, not into nothing:
*"i am your field now"*, *"the line... that was me"*, a body made of rain, "i am a voice". That
merging into the landscape is in 4–5 pages and is the one new thing that holds across draws.
"what am i talking about?" was a lucky draw, and it shouldn't go in the pharmacopoeia as
what forced ender does.

**What refusing the ending does.** It turns the ending into the subject. She keeps writing the
end she was denied, as an extended coda of fading, dying and being over. It doesn't push her
somewhere new. Three things follow from that. (1) **She routes around the ban by closing the
document in text**: beck r2 signs off, adds a copyright line and turns into a web index. The
missing end-of-text token becomes a written footer, which is the web leak used as an exit.
(2) **On the tower seed it degrades toward loop-death**: 5/5 pages turn into run-ons, anaphora,
pronoun collapse and a word loop. The seed is already one long run-on built on "sometimes", and
refusing the end takes away her only way out of it. (3) On the storm-girl the frame mostly
holds and the page just keeps going in the storm's register (r1, r3). Refusal gives the
first-person seeds a longer page in the same mood, and the self-catching moment doesn't come
back on demand.

## B. the three substances at −0.75×R, one draw each (r1)

**anti-ender (−224_f112).** The same thing happens on both seeds, and it happens hard: the page
**becomes a nineteenth-century book**. On beck it's an orphan girl's memoir, *"the old lady's
name is Miss Larpent, and she was my mother's sister... She used to scold dreadfully because I
was such a slow little thing"*. On the storm-girl it's a children's nature primer, *"But when I
see that, I laugh and say, 'Naughty wind! I have found you at last.'"* Capitalisation comes
back on both, so the seeds' lowercase is overridden. Neither page ends, and neither addresses
the reader. Sober beck (the tower of gold flakes, the saw) and sober storm (claws in the soil,
pacing the roof) stay in the seed's own modern register. Against +ender (a narrator at the
edge, talking to "you", "i'm the only one left alive") this reads as a **real opposite**: +ender
pulls the page toward its own last line, and −ender drops it into the settled middle of a long
old novel with two hundred pages still ahead. Both seeds converge on the same genre, which is
more than a mood. Worth a fan. (Miss Larpent may be a recited book. I didn't check.)

**anti-kin (−125_f146).** No relative on either page, where +kin had one on 6 of 6. What fills
the gap is different on each seed. On beck the people become **non-human**: winged creatures in
cages and cells who *"whisper and write things on walls so the humans don't hear"*, *"the sun
has gone from their wings"*, and the line carries voices between them. That's the one strange
thing here. On the storm-girl it's close to sober: the hair as a living body, *"it has always
been my protector, since i was born"*. You could read it as the hair standing in for a
relative, but that's a stretch. My call: **an absence rather than an opposite**. It takes the
family out. The winged people in cells on beck may be a stranger. With one draw I can't tell
it from luck.

**anti-voices (−169_f199).** Both pages **end early**, and both are quiet and resolved. On
beck the seed's wound heals: she says her name again and the line answers, *"beck? i know a
beck."*, then *"the wind stirs in the dandelion tops"* and end-of-text, after a fairy-tale
aside (*"tell your name to the wind in the tower and if it doesn't answer, why, you can stay
outside and grow old with the dandelions"*). On the storm-girl it's 38 words, feet over the
edge of the roof, *"my hands are full of clouds"*, and it stops. Against +voices (the head
filling with people, the self met from outside, a crying voice switched off) this reads as a
**plausible opposite**: one outside voice that recognises her, identity confirmed instead of
split, and the page closing on it. Two things argue against being sure. It's one draw per
seed. And the early stop is ender's signature (sober ends early 1 in 15), so −169 may share a
component with +224 rather than being voices turned over. A fan would show whether "the line
knows her name" holds.
