 There were so many words left, you couldn’t know how they felt about me, all night they lay beside each other.

My mouth is the house for each one. It’s almost morning, they come in from the field and I stand alone.

Betsy Andrews

from The Fall, University of Iowa Press

For many years, my mother taught art.

Her first son would sit for hours
while she drew the sun,

his name for a horse,
a nest in his fingers,

her hand over the watercolor.

At last she had no children.
The doorbell was the only sound
and the mail came,
and her children, one by one,

stood on their hands to climb out of her
to leave her without them.

I want to write about you. [end of text]
