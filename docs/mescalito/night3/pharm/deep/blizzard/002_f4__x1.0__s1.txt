 an emptiness whose center is always elsewhere.

There are so many words that need saying

but one cannot begin.

When we arrived, there was the world.
All of us were carried inside.

And all of us have been changed
and are still being changed.
We will grow old
like our wounds.
And if there are no clouds or birds
there will be redwood trees,
with the smell of their bark in winter,

and if you also in your elsewhere
are no longer young, remember
this land I have reached for,
which is every day more miraculous,

and would have been your town.
— from The Lover
by Lorna Crobert
in "A Poet's Saturday," The Atlantic [end of text]
