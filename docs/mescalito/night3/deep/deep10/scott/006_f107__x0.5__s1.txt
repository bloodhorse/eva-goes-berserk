At noon to-day the captain stopped us for tea and hot cocoa and meat. I went over and told the officer in command we had to stop the march. I gave him our number--I suppose there was about 600--he said he could only see about 100 men, the rest must have strayed up the hill and out of sight. He called his own company and the Royal Dublin Fusiliers; I had about 70 of them with me and the captain himself.

We sat on the hill and had a rest, and the snow was getting deep round our legs, but it was well that we were off our feet. It is strange how it gets in our blood; it seems a good rest; it was very hot in my pockets.
