 know till lunch that I was hurt. There are others worse than me.

The dog was not eaten; it is in his pack. We left the poor brute behind when we turned back. I hope he's alright. The snow is soft; there may be tracks to show where he fell down the crevasse.

We have got rid of one tent and two sleeping bags, so we can now manage.

This evening we reached the depot; have eaten well--two each--and shall turn back after lunch to-morrow. I wonder whether we shall reach One Ton Depot again before our food gives out? That will depend on the wind. We shall keep our course, however, and let the wind take us where it likes. There's a gale coming up again, by the way, for the wind has risen from E.N.E.,
