We did not have to wait long to hear of the fate of our captives; for upon returning to our camp after having completed our journey to the west, we learned that they had been placed in captivity, and that we had taken part in their captivity.

As we came into contact with those Indians, who had come from far away to meet us, we could feel their joy at seeing us arrive, and could sense their relief at having escaped from captivity. The fact that we had arrived was the first indication of the great change in the Indians’ way of life that had been wrought by our arrival, and the fact that we were now able to give them freedom and justice
