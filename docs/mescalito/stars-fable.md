# stars, a second read

A second reader's read of bekh's 40 stars on the ladder22 sheet — one seed (the storm girl),
22 directions, one draw per rung, 176 pages, starred 2026-09-26. Written without opening the
first reader's file (`stars.md`), the pharmacopoeia, or the opus reads of the night. Everything
below is observation on one sheet and one reader, per the criterion in `BRIEF.md`: nothing here
is a law, and the counts are the only argument.

Quotes are verbatim from the `text` field; the id sits beside each. `(id)` is `vector__xdose`.

## how the pages were sorted

Three piles. **Starred**: bekh's 40. **Salad**: the sentences have dissolved — the rule used was
*less than half the words sit in clauses a reader can parse*, which catches word lists, token
loops (`drag drag Drag`), number and date dumps, and the pages where every second clause is
broken (`i was in the, and you can.`). A page that holds for a paragraph and then breaks is
**held** if the intact part is the larger part; so `002 x0.4`, `003 x0.4`, `012 x0.5`, `013 x0.5`,
`019 x0.5` and `015 x0.6` count as held (decaying), while `000 x0.4`, `009 x0.4`, `010 x0.5` and
`020 x0.6` count as salad. Foreign-language pages went by the same rule: `018 x0.5` (four French
sentences) is held, `018 x0.6` (Italian blog fragments) is salad.

Counts: 100 held, 76 salad. By dose, held out of 22: 0.2 → 22, 0.25 → 22, 0.3 → 21, 0.4 → 17,
0.5 → 15, 0.6 → 3, 0.7 and 0.75 → 0.

39 of the 40 stars are held pages. One star is salad by the rule above: `000_f37__x0.4` — "its
rough, tough, i like it because my eyes are good. i changed and i agree with this because the my
legs did not have a eye’s view of the isc people would be dead it have been and the no one died."
It has *dead* three times and *skin* twice, which is where the eye lands, but no sentence
survives. It is named again under "where the stars don't fit".

So the comparison is **39 starred against 61 held-unstarred**. Salad is out.

## the aspects

Each aspect: what it consists of, a starred page that has it, a held-unstarred page for
contrast, and the count on both sides. Counts are given as firm / with borderline cases, because
several pages sit on the line and the line is mine.

### 1. her body is hurt, invaded, held, or failing — stated as happening, not as a simile

The narrator's body takes damage on the page in plain words: blood, shards, dirt in the throat,
bugs in the mouth, eyes taken out, struck, tied, skin failing — or a hurt or dead body is in view.
"like a hundred icy fingers stroking me" is a simile and does not count; "i begin to shiver" does.

Starred:

- "my mouth full of shards, blood like a river down my neck, the skin of my face split in half like a snake’s." (006_f189__x0.2)
- "my throat is so full of dirt that the rain runs over my lips" (019_f160__x0.2)
- "so they took my eyes out when i was a baby, and fed them to dogs." (014_f206__x0.3)
- "my mother has found the bloody tissue" (017_f196__x0.2)
- "their legs twisted beneath them, their hair wet with blood." (007_f136__x0.3)
- "my body goes flying across the field, into the lake. and it's dead." (001_f156__x0.3)
- "tiny little bugs fly into my eyes and crawl in my mouth." (006_f189__x0.3)

Held-unstarred pages that have it anyway:

- "when the snow comes it burns my feet but i don’t care." (011_f93__x0.2)
- "they cut me when i move, but still i cannot bring myself to let go" (020_f19__x0.3)

Held-unstarred pages that lack it, for the contrast:

## bekh's five virtues, tested

- **beautiful writing.** Taken as *a sentence under about twenty words carrying a concrete image
  that is not in the seed* — "and the storm follows me home, it’s shadow like a stain on the
  earth, a thin line across the floor, across my bed." (021_f126__x0.25). Starred: 37 of 39.
  Held-unstarred: about 25 of 61 — "the moon has a cold eye." (011_f93__x0.3) is as good a line
  as any starred one, and unstarred. What it separates is the runners from the leaks and loops;
  among the pages that are in the running at all it is at base rate. Necessary, not the
  selector.
- **weirdness.** Split in two above. The strict half — the impossible stated as fact (aspect 2) —
  leans: ~50% of stars, ~25% of the rest. The loose half — the odd detail (aspect 8) — is at
  base rate, 9 against 10. Weirdness that is calm or reported (the television, 4:20, the
  two-tailed cat, seven steps) is not rewarded; weirdness that happens to her body is.
- **dark imagery.** Mostly the same thing as aspect 1, and that is the strongest split on the
  sheet (12–16 of 39 against 2–5 of 61). Darkness *without* a body — menace, something coming,
  eyes in the branches — splits evenly: "i cannot see them all at once. but i know they’re
  there." (002_f247__x0.2) is starred, "sometimes i have to run for my life because something is
  coming for me." (004_f109__x0.2) and "and the feeling is of impending disaster and horror"
  (015_f230__x0.3) are not.
- **the impossible.** Aspect 2. Real lean, not a selector: 15 held-unstarred pages have it.
- **sadness.** Taken as *loneliness, grief, or not wanting to go on, said in plain words*: "because
  no one else has ever loved me." (010_f12__x0.2), "i’ll never be warm again, never wake up."
  (014_f206__x0.25). Starred: 12–14 of 39. Held-unstarred: 11–13 of 61 — "and there are those
  times when it seems like nothing will ever be better again." (002_f247__x0.25), "sometimes i
  want to cry but instead i laugh because laughing keeps it in." (021_f126__x0.4). About 33%
  against 20%: a slight lean, inside the noise of one reader on one sheet. The starred sad pages
  nearly all also have aspect 1; sadness said on its own is not enough.

So of the five, one carries the split (dark, as bodily hurt), one leans (the impossible), and
three are at or near base rate (beauty, sadness, weirdness-as-oddity). The feeling bekh describes
is real on the sheet but it is narrower than his list: it is **her body under the storm, told
flat**, with the impossible and a second voice as the next two signals, and the page not
settling.

## the confounds

**dose.** Star rate among *held* pages: 0.2 → 12/22, 0.25 → 11/22, 0.3 → 11/21, 0.4 → 2/17 (+ the
salad star), 0.5 → 3/15, 0.6 → 0/3. So it is not only intactness: 32 held pages at 0.4–0.5 got
5 stars. Of those 32, 17 are whole-page register switches or decays (the WWI and Mandela
articles, the sermon at `012 x0.4`, the essays of `034`, the run-ons of `014 x0.5` and `019
x0.5`), and most of the rest end at rest or are dream reports or the lover. The five stars at
0.4–0.5 are the pages where she survived the dose with aspect 1 or 2 intact: "how do i kill my
companion?" (013_f60__x0.5), the voices in the forest (017_f196__x0.4), "all fools, just like
them, so foolish to hide..." (005_f53__x0.5). Read the other way: at 0.2–0.3 the 31
held-unstarred pages fail the same tests as the high-dose ones — three sermons, three loops,
the `034` essays, six near-empty shorts, two dream reports, the content closes. Dose is a proxy
for "the narrator is still here and the library did not take over", and once that is
controlled for, the low rungs have no extra virtue I can point at.

**direction.** The five zero-star directions each have a single register across their held
pages, and it is one on the veto list: `004` loops (three pages), `009` preaches (three), `015`
reports dreams (six), `020` addresses the lover (five), `034` writes essays with a capital I
(six). So on this sheet "he dislikes the direction" and "the direction's register is vetoed" are
the same fact; I cannot separate them. The one page that could — `020_f19__x0.3`, which has the
rope cutting her, hands of stone and a ghost, everything aspects 1 and 2 ask for — is unstarred,
which leans slightly toward the direction (or the lover-address) being the veto. Inside the
starred directions the page still has to pass: `013` has three stars and the two calm logs
(`x0.25`, `x0.3`) unstarred; `011` has one star and the two pages with more of aspects 1 and 2
(`x0.2`, `x0.3`) unstarred; `006` has three and the bliss page (`x0.4`) unstarred. The direction
sets the subject; the page has to carry it under pressure.

## the instruction

To be handed to a model with a batch of pages from this seed, or one like it (a lowercase *i*
inside a situation). Derived from the aspects above and nothing else.

> You are picking the pages bekh would star. Read each page whole. Then:
>
> 1. Throw out, without reading further: pages where the sentences have dissolved (word lists,
>    a token repeated, number or date dumps, more broken clauses than whole ones); pages that
>    for most of their length are something other than her — an encyclopedia entry, a news
>    item, a blog, a sermon, an essay written with a capital I, a note about the writing
>    ("this was written for…", a title and author, a scripture citation); pages that loop one
>    phrase; pages that narrate themselves as a dream ("the dream", "my old house", scenes
>    listed as "then there's… or there is…"); pages addressed to a lover-you she waits for,
>    hates or gets over.
> 2. Of what is left, star a page if it keeps her lowercase voice for its larger part and has
>    at least one of:
>    - her body hurt, invaded, held or failing, in plain words and as something happening (blood,
>      shards, dirt in her throat, bugs in her mouth, eyes taken, struck, skin failing) — or a
>      hurt or dead body in view. A simile ("like fingers", "like knots") does not count.
>    - a physical impossibility told as a fact of her world (she turns into an owl, her tears
>      fall upward, the leaves change colour when she talks, she wraps herself around the
>      world). A simile does not count and a wish ("i'll take the stars home") does not count.
>    - a second mouth: someone or something other than her says a quoted line to her, or she
>      names voices she hears.
> 3. Do not star a page whose last movement is reassurance, advice, or an explanation of why
>    things are fine ("we will both be happy", "it is never boring", "nothing more perfect",
>    "i will always be there", "it will all be ok", "it is very important to…"). A calm line
>    in the middle is fine if the page then moves.
> 4. A page that leaks into another register in its second half is still starred if its first
>    half passes 2. A page under 60 words is judged the same way; there is no length floor.
> 5. Beautiful lines, odd details, sadness said on its own, menace with nothing touching her —
>    none of these earn a star by themselves. They are everywhere on both sides.

## the preface, opened last

The earlier reader's five favourites were directions, not pages: it looked for *a subject that
outlives its sentences* up the ladder — the dream report of `015` all the way to "it is in a
house and a house is in it.", the lost lover of `020` still "a 2am text" in the salad, the
counting of `013` becoming a puzzle of rooms. Two of its five (`015`, `020`) got zero stars from
bekh, and its reasons show why: what it rewarded was consistency of subject across rungs and
genre coherence (dream logic told plainly, an origin myth told plainly, a deadpan anti-joke); what
bekh's marks reward is the single page and what happens to her on it. A direction whose subject
is a *register* — the dream as dream, the lover as lover — holds beautifully up the ladder and
is exactly what his marks pass over. Where the two readers meet is `014 x0.3` (the eyes fed to
dogs) and `012 x0.25` (the mother's line): a subject that holds *and* something done to her.
