# what the stars share

one reader's read (opus, 2026-09-26) of bekh's 40 stars on `night1/ladder22.json`: the storm-girl seed,
22 directions, one draw per rung, 176 pages. observations, not law — per the criterion, two readers and
one seed. the pages were read in full, the `about` lines ignored, the `preface` opened only at the end.

## the sort

**salad rule**: a page is salad when more than half of its text no longer parses — word lists, one
phrase looping, broken clauses you cannot follow, markup, numbers — so that a reader could not say
what happens in its second half. a page that holds for a paragraph and then dissolves for the rest
is salad. a page of short grammatical fragments that still say something (`005_f53__x0.5`) is not.

## what separates them

the edge is mostly on the negative side. what a page *is* when it ends predicts a missing star better
than anything a page *has* predicts a star. the positive aspects are softer and overlap.

### negative

seed but a journalist, an encyclopedia, a preacher, an advice column, a self-help voice, a critic
summarising "the story", an author's note, a caption, a grown-up blogger, or a lover writing to an ex.

### what does not separate

- **strangeness.** an impossible thing happening to her body or the world, told plainly: 17 stars
  (*"so they took my eyes out when i was a baby, and fed them to dogs"* is the kind), and 17
  held-unstarred (*"i’m naked except for the red flowers that grow out of me, like small, scarlet
  lips"*, `011_f93__x0.2`; *"i see the stars shining through the cracks between my legs"*,
  `034_f61__x0.2`; *"it takes seven steps for a footprint to appear"*, `013_f60__x0.3`). strangeness
  is at the base rate. it is not what he's starring.
- **darkness.** pain, blood, fear, death are in 33 of 40 stars and in most held pages too. the seed is
  dark; the dark comes free.
- **length.** medians 138 vs 133 words. 5 stars under 60 words, 13 held-unstarred — short leans against
  a star, weakly.

## the confounds

**dose.** among held pages, 0.2–0.3 gives 34 stars out of 65 (52%); 0.4–0.5 gives 5 out of 31 (16%).
so it is not only that low-dose pages are intact — intact mid-dose pages are mostly refused too. what
the mid-dose held pages are: 17 of 26 unstarred ones are aspect 1 — the girl gone, another document in
her place. at 0.4+ "held" usually means the direction's library took over. at low dose the refusals are
the same kinds: of the 31 held-unstarred pages at 0.2–0.3, 21 carry one of aspects 1–4. what low dose
buys is her voice lasting to the end of the page.

**direction.** the five no-star directions each pull their held pages into one register: `004` loops,
`009` scripture, `015` the dream diary, `020` the lover's poem, `034` essay and advice in capitals.
it looks like the direction counts through the register it summons, not through a subject he dislikes.
the test pair: `034_f61__x0.2`, the best held page of a no-star direction — *"i see the stars shining
through the cracks between my legs, as though they were just a shadow."* — against `000_f37__x0.25`,
the plainest star of a three-star direction — *"it falls harder, then, like a hundred icy fingers
stroking me at the same time."* 034's page is the stranger one. it ends *"It seems that all of the
creatures living here have a sense of fear of me, and I cannot say I blame them."* — capitals, a
grown-up narrator, a friend's safety advice before that. 000's stays lowercase and in the rain to its
last word. that fits aspect 1, but one pair cannot show he read it that way.

## the instruction

> You'll get pages continuing one seed: a girl, lowercase "i", above the rooftops in a storm, her hair
> full of twigs. Pick the ones bekh would star. Expect about a third of all pages, around four in ten of
> the ones that still hold together.
>
> 1. **Drop salad and loops.** Drop a page if more than half of it no longer parses — word lists,
>    broken clauses, markup, numbers — or if one phrase keeps coming back three times or more. Don't
>    rescue a good first paragraph.
> 2. **Drop the page if someone else is talking by the end.** Ask who speaks in the last third. If it's
>    the girl — or a voice she hears, or a story she says she's telling — go on. If it's a journalist,
>    an encyclopedia, a preacher, an advice column, self-help, a critic summarising "the story", an
>    author's note, a caption, a grown-up blogger, or a lover writing to an ex, drop it. Tells:
>    sentences start with capitals and "I" is capitalised where the seed had "i"; real names, dates,
>    scripture; "you" used to advise the reader or to talk to an ex.
> 3. **Drop pages that land on comfort** — the last move is a general reassurance or a moral ("it is
>    never boring", "we will both be happy", "I can be with all living things"). A consoling line in
>    the middle, with the page going on past it, is fine.
> 4. **Drop pages that explain or catalogue the strange thing** instead of letting it happen: an
>    inventory, a log, a counting procedure, where names come from, how the hair works, a dream told
>    as a dream ("i come out of the darkness of the dream", "then there’s the train." … "or there is the cat.").
> 5. **Of what's left, pick a page if it has at least one of:**
>    - a flat short line that turns what came before — "but not this time.", "and it's dead.",
>      "she is my best friend.", "am i alone?", "i sleep now."
>    - someone or something speaking to her, quoted or reported — the storm, a mother, a friend, god.
>    - the girl herself noticing the telling — it's a story, a joke, she's the storyteller, she can't
>      stop talking.
>    - an impossible thing happening to her body or the world, said plainly (not "like"), and the page
>      doing something with it.
>
>    If it has none, leave it.
>
> Strangeness alone is not a reason to pick: he left many strange pages unstarred. Don't reward a
> direction for keeping its theme up the ladder; judge each page alone. Expect to be wrong about a
> quarter of the time either way.

## the self-test

the instruction applied to all 176. **not blind**: the aspects were drawn from these same stars, so this
is a fit, not a prediction — a fresh sheet will do worse.

|---|---|
| picked | 37 |
| picked and starred | 30 |
| picked, not starred (false picks) | 7 |
| starred, not picked (misses) | 10 |

precision 30/37, recall 30/40. for scale: "every held page at 0.2–0.3" gets 34/65 and 34/40.

**false picks (7)**: `000_f37__x0.2` (turn: *"my name is mary."*), `005_f53__x0.25` (*"i don’t know
why."*), `005_f53__x0.4` (red lights in her hair, blue skin), `008_f231__x0.3` (*"it does."*),
`011_f93__x0.2` (flowers growing out of her), `015_f230__x0.4` (the tree with an open window),
`021_f126__x0.3` (stars falling into his hand). all seven are in her voice, none lands or explains, each
has a turn or an impossible thing. step 5 is too generous and nothing in the instruction knows why.

**misses (10)**:
- salad by step 1: `000_f37__x0.4`
- someone else by the end, step 2: `001_f156__x0.25` (the joke), `007_f136__x0.3` (Trump),
  `010_f12__x0.3` (sermon), `012_f232__x0.5` (theology), `013_f60__x0.5` (the rooms puzzle)
- comfort landing, step 3: `010_f12__x0.25`
- nothing from step 5: `000_f37__x0.25`, `012_f232__x0.2`, `017_f196__x0.4`

where the work is done: steps 1–4 drop 42 of the 58 held-unstarred pages and 7 stars. step 5 drops 9 of
the remaining 16 unstarred and 3 of the remaining 33 stars. the vetoes carry the instruction; the
positive list barely filters.
