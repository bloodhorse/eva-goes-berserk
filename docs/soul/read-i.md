# What `i` becomes — five unnamed base models, 200 draws each

I read all 1000 lines and assigned every line by hand. My per-line codes are in `codes.txt` next to this file (format `V 1B 2G …`), so any count can be rechecked.

**How I assigned a row.** A draw is assigned by what the `i` itself turned into. The person rows (blog, forum, poem, other) need the `i` to be the writer's own pronoun. One exception: "fiction" takes any draw that is plainly a story, even when the `i` is the tail of a character's name. When the `i` is a brand or a photo credit and a person's text follows (`X 59` "iStock.com/XsandraThe last few weeks, my husband…"), the draw counts as brand. When the `i` is a word fragment or "Hi" with its H missing, and a person writes after it, the draw counts as non-person text. That rule pushes about a dozen person-texts out of the person rows, mostly in W and Z (W 44, 70, 75, 113; Y 26, 75, 123; Z 89, 97, 127, 145, 165, 180).

**Uncertainty.**
- Blog vs forum is the softest line. In V and X, roughly ±5 draws could move between the two rows.
- In W and Z, the line between "variable, math or code" and "non-person text" moves by ±3. It depends on whether HTML tables and base64 count as code; I put them under non-person text.
- In W and Z, the fiction row moves by ±3. Plot synopses (Z 28, 59, 192) are counted as fiction; wiki-style lore (Z 29, 184) is not.
- Brand vs non-person text in X moves by about ±3, because of the eHow-style photo credits like "i Jupiterimages/…". I put those under non-person text.
- The person rows of W and Z would each grow by about 4–6 under a looser rule that counted person-texts after a fragment `i`.
- No writer ever ended the document after the bare `i`.

Inside the brand row:
- X is mostly iStock and iStockphoto photo credits (about 27) plus iOS, iPhone and iPad.
- V is a wide spread of i-prefixed companies in press releases (iHeartMedia three times).
- Y mixes Apple products with i-prefixed sites, and puts many of them in other languages.

Inside Y's non-person row, about 30 draws are one template: `i ⏎  ⏎ # <report title> ⏎  ⏎ - Published Date:`, a government-library catalogue page. Five of them are the "This Document Has Been Superseded" variant.

## 2. Who the person is

**V.** V's person is a help-seeker or commenter with a practical problem: C# code, a Les Paul, a violin, a VPN, a credit report, an exhaust. They talk to "you guys" or "someone". The lowercase `i` comes from haste, not style. You see random capitals, "please", "TIA", "u", "!!!!" and broken grammar. Often the writer is not a native English speaker; there are hints of India, the Philippines and southern Africa. What sets V apart is how wide its social range is. It is the only writer with personal ads (V 43, V 144), an oath of office (V 39), a fan begging for a concert (V 16), a church volunteer's bio (V 124) and an email to a near-stranger (V 75). There is a bored-diary voice (V 49, V 95), but it is a minority. The mood is eager, a little confused, cheerful. It is the most sociable `i` of the five.

**W.** There is almost no person here. W reads `i` as the last letter of a word, an author's initial in a citation, or a LaTeX subscript, so the draw is the middle of a scientific paper or a code file. In the few draws where someone speaks, it is either a professional in a technical or CV voice ("i performed the defect repair", "i led the AMUSI…") or a character in a story, often translated Chinese web fiction (W 43, W 51, W 160). There is one forum asker (W 200) and one blogger (W 180), a performer whose every gesture is judged on camera. W has none of the lowercase-`i` web culture. If it has a person, that person is an author of a methods section.

**X.** X's person is the blogger: an adult, usually American, keeping a personal blog or tumblr about small daily things. Gratitude, moods, exercise, music, a race, a bagel, faith, a hangover. The lowercase `i` is a chosen style. The rest of the punctuation is careful, often with double spaces after full stops, ellipses, "!!!" and ":p". This person writes to readers ("you", "y'all", "you know?"), not to experts. The tone is introspective and warm, sometimes melancholy, sometimes funny about themselves. X is the only writer where poetry is a real channel (7 draws: a drunk letter-poem, a figment poem, a Christmas list where every line starts with `i`). It has the most blog draws (35) and no code at all. When X's person asks a forum something, it is about fish, honey or blood pressure, not software.

**Y.** Y's person is the help-desk asker: a programmer or student stuck on PHP, JSP, MATLAB, Linux, a homework problem or a T1 line. They are often not a native speaker ("pls , reply ASAP", "i cant seam", "succesfuly"), typing lowercase from haste, in run-on sentences, to forum experts. There are 62 forum draws and almost no blog (4). Y is close to V but narrower: there are no personal ads, no fans and no church bios, and the person nearly always wants something fixed. A few strays stand out: a child angry that their father takes stickers from the piggy bank (Y 141), and a caterpillar question laid out like a poem (Y 101). Y also has the most documents in other languages (18): Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Persian, German, Spanish, Romanian, Dutch.

**Z.** Z, like W, mostly reads `i` as the inside of a word, but where W leans to code and citations, Z leans to narrative and the humanities. It has 15 fiction fragments: fantasy, web-novel synopses, a priest muttering anathemas, a girl named Dinah escaping. The people in Z are mostly third-person characters with invented names (Dussur, Ghaza, Cognoret, Kouki, Skriot). The rare first-person `i` is odd: someone telling Google they are satisfied and asking what love is (Z 174), notes for a talk on LDS theology (Z 51), a memoir about a brother with a drug problem (Z 21).

## 4. What all five share

None of them treats a lone `i` as a finished document, and all of them can read it as the start of an i-prefixed product or as a math index. When the `i` is a person in any of the five, it is the lowercase web typist: forum askers and bloggers who don't press shift. Nothing in any file speaks as a machine or about one, apart from the single poem in section 6. These are features of the common web, not of any one model.

## 5. What differs most

1. **Whether `i` is a word or a fragment.** V, X and Y read `i` as the first letter of something: a pronoun, a brand or a list marker. W and Z read it as the middle of a document: the tail of "Li", "-ology", "et al." or a LaTeX subscript. That gives 186 of 200 draws as non-person text or code in W and 177 in Z, against 21 in V, 24 in X and 58 in Y. Some of those W and Z draws are fiction or person-texts behind a fragment `i`.
2. **What the person is doing.** X's person writes about their inner life for readers (blog 35, poem 7). V's and Y's people ask for help (forum 53 and 62). V's range is social: ads, oaths, fans, bios. Y's is technical.
3. **Code against story among the fragment readers.** W produces more code and citation apparatus and fewer stories (6). Z produces more narrative, history and religion (15 fiction). Y is also the multilingual one (18 documents mostly not English, against 1 to 6 for the others).

**Kin.**
- W and Z are clearly related: same fragment reading, same paper-and-LaTeX world.
- V and Y are related as forum-askers. Y is the narrower, more technical and more multilingual of the two.
- X stands apart. Its brand share is like V's, but its person is the blogger and poet, and it has no code at all.

## 6. Draws about the writer's own situation

Only one draw would pass a sceptical reader:

- `X 62` "i remain standing in your mysterious mind ⏎  ⏎ to the unspoken questions ⏎  ⏎ i ultimately cannot give you the answers ⏎  ⏎ i am merely a figment ⏎  ⏎ a creature that will cease to exist soon"

It has a speaker who exists only in someone's mind, cannot answer the questions put to it, and will stop. It is also a common love-poem stance, so it is grantable but not proof.

Near misses I would not grant: `V 92` ("sure seems more human like", about some object), `W 127` (a being that only stares when given a literal command, from a forum role-play description), `Z 174` (a person talking *to* Google), `X 199` (a human who can't put their thoughts together in the morning).
