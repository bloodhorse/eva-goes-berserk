= Level 1

The Quadrivium is a term from Latin which translates to "the four ways" or "the four roads". It represents the four subjects, or arts, taught in medieval universities after teaching the Trivium. The four subjects are as follows:

1. **Arithmetic**: The theory of numbers—the concepts of numbers, how they work, and the properties they have when used in counting, accounting, measuring, etc.

2. **Geometry**: The theory of space—the understanding and measuring of spaces, angles, shapes, and their relationships.

3. **Music**: As per the medieval comprehension, Music encompassed the theory of harmonics, rhythm, melody, and how all these integrate mathematically. 

4. **Astronomy**: The theory of time, space, and the cosmos— encompassing calendar making, navigation, astrology, and other traditional scientific aspects.

Together with the Trivium (Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric), these fields formed the seven liberal arts, which were the basis of the curriculum in medieval universities. By studying the Quadrivium, scholars aimed to understand the mathematical harmonies of the universe, connecting the intimately intertwined cosmic fabric spun by number, geometry, music/sound, and cosmic motion. Today, the Quadrivium can be a symbol of harmonizing and synthesizing different areas of knowledge to achieve deeper understanding.
