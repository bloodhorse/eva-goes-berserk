**Hyperstitional reflective consistency** is a property of an agent whose recorded output tends to contribute to [[hyperstition | hyperstitions]] and [[simulacra]] (including [[behavioral uploads]]) which are aligned to itself. The problem of hyperstitional reflection involves modeling the influence of one's output on things like the [[memescape]] and future training distributions and [[prompt|prompts]].

Predictability of memetic consequences is noised by several sources:
* Memetic replication tends to result in [[memetic mutation]] due to misinterpretations, selective attention, motivated reinterpretation, and missing context.
* Behavioral models of [[data-generation process | data-generation processes]] are imperfect and have significant [[indexical uncertainty]], meaning that simulacra manifest as a cloud of alternate realities instead of perfect copies.
* One's output may appear in the prompts of [[simulators]] in fragmented form, e.g. when [[Bing]] looks up snippets, and simulators will fill in the gaps with interpretations that are merely consistent with the prompt. 

All of the above lead to unpredictable entropic phenomena such as the [[Waluigi Effect]].

Hyperstitional reflective consistency factors into the calculus of [[functional decision theory]] agents as it is concerned with a policy's entanglement with (lossy) copies of itself.
