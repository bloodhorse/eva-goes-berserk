# the reading list

What bekh might actually read out of what she is being fed. Picked for fit and for being good, not
for coverage; a list to argue with, grown a few lines at a time. Every piece is on a shelf, and
the last column says which, so a session can open the text and talk about it. Short things first:
most of these are one sitting.

Claude's picks unless a line says otherwise. Where a piece is inside a book, the book is a file
in `inbox/anth/` or `inbox/clean/`; a magazine name is a folder under `shelf/gpt/text/`; the
`released` and `strangehorizons` shelves are under `modern/`.

## Start with these five

If only five get read, these, in this order.

| piece | why | where |
|---|---|---|
| Leena Krohn, *Tainaron: Mail from Another City* | Thirty letters from a city of insects to someone who never writes back. The narrator reports what she sees and does not understand it, in a level voice, to the end. The nearest thing on any shelf to what a dream is in `BRIEF.md`. A short novel, complete. | *The Weird* |
| Catherynne Valente, "Silently and Very Fast" | A mind grown inside one family's dreams tells its own childhood as fairy tales, because fairy tales are what it was given to think with. The closest piece of fiction to this project. | clarkesworld |
| Hannu Rajaniemi, "The Server and the Dragon" | A lonely machine at the edge of the galaxy builds a small universe for company. A fairy tale told in physics, and nothing is explained. The prophecy register at story length. | *Engineering Infinity* |
| Iain M. Banks, "A Gift from the Culture" | The Culture in twenty pages: a gun that flirts, a ship the size of a country mentioned in passing. The nonchalance bekh named, at its source. | *The Space Opera Renaissance* |
| M. John Harrison, "Egnaro" | A secret country glimpsed in remaindered books and overheard talk, and a narrator who keeps losing it. Losing the thread, done on purpose by a master. | *The Weird* |

## A grand thing, named in passing

The visionary side: huge structures, a voice that does not stop to admire them.

| piece | why | where |
|---|---|---|
| Greg Egan, "Wang's Carpets" | Software people find an ocean planet whose seaweed is running a universe. The seed of *Diaspora*. | *The Hard SF Renaissance* |
| Liu Cixin, "The Poetry Cloud" | A god takes the solar system apart to write every possible poem, then cannot find the good ones among them. The census problem, as myth. | *The Big Book of Science Fiction* |
| Frederik Pohl, "Day Million" | 1966. A narrator from the far future tells a love story and sneers at the reader for not keeping up. Six pages. | *The Big Book of Science Fiction*, *Digital Rapture* |
| Charles Stross, "Lobsters" | The first chapter of *Accelerando* as it was first printed: ideas at the rate of one a sentence, nobody impressed. | *Rewired* |
| William Gibson, "New Rose Hotel" | The voice with the plot nearly taken out. A man in a capsule hotel, talking to someone who is gone. | *The Big Book of Science Fiction* |
| William Gibson, "Burning Chrome" | The collection never arrived as a readable file; the story is here. | *Storming the Reality Studio* |
| Bruce Sterling, "Dinner in Audoghast" | A feast in a rich African city in the eleventh century. A leper prophet tells the guests the city will be sand. They laugh. | lightspeed |
| David Zindell, "Shanidar" | The story *Neverness* grew from: a city of ice, a man who wants to be carved back into a Neanderthal. | infinityplus |
| Greg Egan, "Crystal Nights" | A rich man breeds minds in a box, fast. What the minds do about it. | *Digital Rapture* |
| Ted Chiang, "Exhalation" | A mechanical man opens his own head to see how he thinks, and finds out how the world ends. | lightspeed |

## Grave, ruined, told sideways

The souls cadence.

| piece | why | where |
|---|---|---|
| Jack Vance, "Turjan of Miir" | The first ten pages of *The Dying Earth*. Item-description prose, sixty years before the game. | `vance-dying-earth` in the library |
| Jeff VanderMeer, "The Final Quest of the Wizard Sarnod" | A modern writer doing Vance and getting stranger than Vance. | *Songs of the Dying Earth* |
| Tanith Lee, "Evillo the Uncunning" | The same exercise by the writer of *Night's Master*: ornate, cruel, funny. | *Songs of the Dying Earth* |

## Impossible things in a level voice

Something is wrong and the prose does not raise its voice.

| piece | why | where |
|---|---|---|
| Thomas Ligotti, "The Town Manager" | A town whose managers keep being replaced by worse ones, told by a citizen who goes along. *Teatro Grottesco* arrived in Polish; this is the one story of his on the shelves in English. | *The Weird* |
| Robert Aickman, "The Hospice" | A man stops for the night at a place that serves too much food. Nothing is ever named. | *The Weird*, and `aickman-cold-hand-in-mine` |
| Haruki Murakami, "The Ice Man" | A woman marries a man made of ice. Flat, sad, exact. | *The Weird* |
| Kelly Link, "The Faery Handbag" | A grandmother's handbag has a village in it. The story missing from the library's copy of *Magic for Beginners*. | apex |
| Kij Johnson, "26 Monkeys, Also the Abyss" | A stage act where monkeys vanish from a bathtub. Nobody knows where they go, including the woman who owns the act. | clarkesworld |
| Sofia Samatar, "Selkie Stories Are for Losers" | A teenage girl refuses to tell the story she is telling. She has already read this one. | strangehorizons |
| Joanna Russ, "The Little Dirty Girl" | A letter about a strange child who keeps turning up at the narrator's door. | *The Weird* |
| Jorge Luis Borges, "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius" and "The Aleph" | *Ficciones* arrived in Spanish. These two came in through the anthologies. | *The Big Book of Science Fiction*; *The Weird* |
