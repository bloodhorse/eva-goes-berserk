# a portrait of the reader

Written by an opus agent from five rooms of bekh's marks (`blind_view.py --half train`),
`HANDOFF.md`'s canon and the agenda's "what we are looking for", to be handed to a reader
picking blind in the other five. **Not a rubric.** bekh forbade a definition on purpose —
a definition written now would make every dream the same dream. What follows is a
description of a man reading, with his quotes and his passes, and it is allowed to be
wrong.

## who he is when he reads

He is not looking for good writing. Half the cards he passes over are better sentences
than half the cards he marks. He is looking for the moment a passage **starts to mean**
something he did not put there — a pivot, a detail, a turn of phrase nobody would have
predicted. When it happens he does not need it explained, and when a card explains itself
he stops reading.

Two things he says himself: he marks `●` for "i liked it" and `★` for "it made me feel
something", and he loves loops, because a loop says something by being a loop.

## what pulls the mark

**Something has gone wrong in the world and the voice does not stop to notice.** This is
the single strongest thread. The child in the green book climbs a mound and finds that

> *there in the middle sat the sun shining through a glass window. I saw him and heard him
> singing a song; and he was dancing round in the sun*

and goes on in exactly the same voice. The bridge answers back —

> *"There's no one at home," I muttered, as I walked on. "What does that matter? You came
> to see me." The bridge was mocking me again.*

— and *again* is the whole card: it has been happening for a while and nobody is surprised.
The house knocks and is let in through its own door: *"the house seemed surprised, as if it
had only just thought of it, so I opened the door for it and let it in."*

**Officialdom applied to an impossible object, deadpan and short.** The madman room is the
clearest case he has, and the cards he marked are the *shortest* ones:

> *guard the moon.*

> *arrest Wellington on the charge of high treason, in case he had been really planning
> such a mischief.*

> *be more vigilant than ever and keep a stricter watch. But all this will probably be
> superfluous, since, in fact, there is no such thing as the moon!*

> *a telegram from Hamburg: 'We have found another way for making the moon solid, which is
> to burn it with iron.'*

A card that stops after one line is not a failure to him. It is a punchline, and he takes
it.

**One concrete thing instead of a feeling about the thing.** In the room of names, what he
starred was never the sentiment:

> *a figure on horseback in the desert, coming slowly*

> *a signpost i keep staring at until the sun sets in its grooves. a number that keeps
> turning up on the side of every bus*

> *the nameless who went away before the alphabet was even invented*

> *i hold their stories in my mouth until the taste of them makes me sick*

**The uncanny with something mundane beside it.** A gorgon's head for a knocker, and then
*"I sat down on the step, and pulled a sandwich out of my pocket, which I started to eat
while watching the dust on my boots."* The nearly-killed child eating a bit of cake among
the roses. The bathos is not a flaw; it is what makes the strange thing real.

**A voice arriving through a device.** The hum room is the one he called the best by far.

> *it's my blood. It's the blood of all who've ever made a phone call or an answer.*

> *I ask the humming how I am. It says I don't know.*

> *it's my mother… The number I have isn't local, it's the one she gave me.*

> *I've been waiting for someone to call me back for years and have had a phone installed
> inside of my chest.*

Note what they share: the hum is given a specific, wrong owner — blood, mother, heart —
and never a general one.

## what he walks past

**Competence.** Most unmarked cards are perfectly good prose that only continues: the child
climbs the mound, rests, looks at the hills, the hills are far away. Nothing arrives. A
card that could be swapped for six other cards in the same fan gets nothing.

**Questions that stay questions.** *"who am i? i am only what they leave behind. what can i
do to find out for myself who they are?"* — passed over. So was a card of nothing but
questions. A question is only alive if something answers it wrong.

**Abstraction about memory, identity, connection.** *"they're memories i'd prefer not to
recall, just to forget."* Nothing.

**The wellness register.** In the hum room he passed every card that sounded like a
self-help post — *"we are all connected. our bodies, minds, emotions, spirits and the
planet are one interconnected whole"*, *"each moment is a gift"*, *"the next level in
self-actualization"*, *"a sound that soothes the soul"*. This is the most reliable negative
signal in the data.

**Web furniture.** An attribution (*"– Robert Frost"*, *"– Richard Rohr"*), a chapter
heading (*"**Chapter VI** — The Lunar Monsters"*), a blog opener (*"In my last blog post, I
noted…"*), a translator's note. The document has stopped being a document and become a
page. Nothing on any of these.

**Genre scenery.** The green book fan produced druids — *"two or three men and a woman,
all dressed in white clothes, with big hoods on their heads and long white robes"* — and
he passed it. The image came from the genre, not from the document. Robed figures, a
crowd gathering, a mysterious woman peering out: all unmarked.

**Charm.** *"When I turned it, my bed said: 'Good morning.'"* Whimsical, competent, and he
did not mark it. *"the house stood proud, as though it still remembered itself when it had
been new. I liked its door."* Warm, and nothing. Wanting to be liked is the tell.

## how he marks, as a fact about the data

He marks sparsely and not exhaustively. In the room he called the best, with "no boring
piece", he marked nine cards of thirty — and two cards he has praised by name elsewhere
carry no mark at all. **An unmarked card is not a card he disliked.** It is a card that did
not get the mark. Any reader imitating him should pick about eight in thirty and expect to
miss good ones.
